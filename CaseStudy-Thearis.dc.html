Thearis · Solo, AI-assisted

From a broad finance app to a dashboard that tells a first-time freelancer what to do next

Thearis is an AI-assisted financial dashboard for independent earners. It began as a women-focused finance app and kept trying to serve everyone at once. I helped narrow it to a single sharp user, first-time independent earners, and reframed the product around a dashboard-first first screen that surfaces what matters before the user has to ask. Overview

Thearis helps freelancers, creators, and gig workers make sense of income that arrives irregularly. The product had ambition but no edge; it was trying to be a finance app for everyone. I focused the work on the people least served by existing tools: first-time independent earners who have never had to manage taxes, buffers, or slow months on their own. My scope was research, the requirements for the landing dashboard, usability evaluation of dashboard variants, and the stakeholder deliverables that moved the decision forward.

Impact

  • Reframed the product around a single ICP, first-time independent earners, giving the team a clear user to design and prioritize against.
  • Defined a dashboard-first model that surfaces proactive guidance before search or chat, backed by Reddit research on how often this user actually checks in.
  • Delivered an 18-slide stakeholder deck with a speaker script that aligned the team on direction. [Add usability results or adoption metrics once available. No invented numbers.]

The challenge

The product had already pivoted once, from a women-focused finance app to independent earners broadly, and “broadly” was the problem. A freelancer three years in and someone who just took their first contract have almost nothing in common. Every feature request pulled the design in a different direction, and the first screen was trying to answer every question at once. The hard part was not adding features; it was choosing who to disappoint, and building a first screen that felt calm to a person who is anxious about money.

The design question

How might we make a first-time freelancer's first screen tell them what to do next, without overwhelming them?

Key decisions

The core of the work. Each one changed the product's direction.

01

Choosing one user out of everyone

“Independent earners broadly” meant no one was designed for. Features fought each other.
Decision
I pushed to narrow the ICP to first-time independent earners and brought stakeholder discovery questions to pressure-test the pivot with Claire.
Why it mattered
A specific user made prioritization possible: we could finally say no to features and yes to guidance.

02

Dashboard before chat

Problem
A chat-first product assumes the user knows what to ask. A first-timer usually doesn't.
Decision
I argued for a dashboard-first model (proactive data and next steps on the landing screen) and wrote the requirements for the “My Canvas” page.
Why it mattered
It shifts the burden from the user to the product; the app does the noticing.
Result
Reddit research confirmed this user checks in irregularly and wants a status read at a glance, which supported the hierarchy. [Add usability finding.]
Visual: dashboard hierarchy

03

Revealing complexity slowly

Problem
Every capability shown at once reads as a demand. A nervous new earner bounces.
Decision
Progressive disclosure: the first screen shows a small, calm set of moves; depth appears as the user is ready. This also shaped the free vs. premium onboarding split.
Why it mattered
Calm is a feature for this user. Fewer choices up front means they actually take the first step.

Reflection

The biggest lever wasn't a screen; it was the ICP. Once we named who we were for, everything downstream got easier to decide. I also directed AI tooling (Claude and Lovable) as part of the workflow: it let me move from research to working screens fast, but the judgment about what to build stayed mine.
